Quantum Predictive Analytics in Disease Forecasting and Epidemiology: A Critical Synthesis of Hybrid Methods, Barriers, and Emerging Applications

ABSTRACT
The intersection of quantum computing and clinical predictive analytics has attracted growing research attention, yet the empirical record remains markedly mixed and is frequently misrepresented through simulator-only benchmarks, under-tuned classical comparators, and the systematic absence of statistical uncertainty reporting. This systematic scoping review evaluates quantum and hybrid quantum–classical methods applied to disease prediction and epidemic modelling, synthesising approximately 160 peer-reviewed studies retrieved from PubMed, Scopus, IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, and arXiv (January 2018 – February 2026) using a PRISMA 2020-adapted protocol (Page et al., 2021).
Across cardiovascular, oncological, neurological, and metabolic disease domains — and across epidemic modelling and precision bioinformatics — quantum and hybrid models have, under specific and often simulator-only conditions, matched or narrowly exceeded classical baselines on tasks including cardiac risk stratification, brain-tumour classification, and county-level epidemic trajectory forecasting (Heidari and Hellstern, 2022; Jenber Belay et al., 2024; He et al., 2025). No systematic review of quantum machine learning (QML) in digital health has, however, found a consistent performance differential sufficient to establish empirical quantum utility at scale (Wang et al., 2025). Of the 160 included studies, fewer than 8% reported results from real quantum hardware; fewer than 20% reported confidence intervals or multi-seed variance; and strong classical comparators — gradient-boosted trees, tuned deep networks — were absent from the majority of benchmarks. These figures, reported here for the first time as a quantitative characterisation of the field's evidentiary base, are themselves the primary finding.
Four barrier categories slow clinical translation: hardware noise and limited circuit depth in the noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era (Chen et al., 2023); trainability pathologies — particularly barren plateaus where gradient variance decays exponentially with qubit count (McClean et al., 2018); data encoding overheads that consume circuit depth before learning executes; and an unresolved regulatory pathway for quantum-enabled Software as a Medical Device (FDA, 2025; Muehlematter et al., 2021). The most credible near-term applications lie in optimisation tasks — vaccination prioritisation, epidemic cohort control, and molecular simulation — where problem structure aligns naturally with quantum primitives. Genuine clinical deployment requires noise-aware benchmarking with confidence intervals, pre-registered protocols, competitive classical baselines, and domain-specific ansatz design. The mathematical ideas underpinning quantum computing are already demonstrably useful in biomedicine; the hardware is not yet the appropriate vehicle for most clinical prediction tasks.
